Boundaries before refactors

1Add the rule as a warning
2Count the violations
3Fix them in batches
4Make it an error

Refactor first, and the codebase erodes back behind you. Make the rule executable first, and every improvement stays made.

What a BFF is for

It is

  • APIs shaped for one frontend
  • The owner of the session
  • Auth and tenancy at the edge
  • An aggregator of platform services

It isn't

A second business-logic tier.

It adapts, combines and protects.

You disagree about an API shape

Their proposal

Offset pagination. Or return everything, and let the frontend filter.

Your concern

Unstable pages under inserts, cost at depth, payloads that grow with the tenant.

Not a debate to win. A decision to make, and record.

Resolve it on the contract

1Understand their constraints
2A PR on openapi.yaml, both shapes
3Agree the criteria first
4Reversible or irreversible?
5The BFF can adapt the shape
6The owner decides; commit; ADR

Criteria, ready-made

ConcernChoiceWhy
ErrorsRFC 9457 problem+json, with traceIdOne error shape for every endpoint
PaginationKeyset (cursor), ordered by (title, id)Stable under inserts, and constant cost at any depth, unlike OFFSET
TenancyThe tenant comes from the token; another tenant's resource is a 404A 403 would confirm that the resource exists

A new feature folder is covered automatically: featureNames() reads the folder.

The decision lint can't make

Presentational?

Down into the design system, with plain inputs.

Behaviour?

Loading courses, say: down into core.

Needs its internals?

Maybe it isn't a new feature at all.

Consider: naming the folder after what the learner does, not the screen.

Raising the bar

You haven't raised the bar. You've become a queue.

Scale yourself out

  • Tooling says no, so you don't have to
  • Reference implementations: the catalog store (ADR-0004)
  • Templates for pull requests and ADRs
  • Spread review ownership across several people
  • Accessibility and tests in the definition of done

Three things make it land

"I" and "we"

"I" for your decisions, "we" for the team's work.

Sequencing

Each sprint shipped something on its own.

A number

One you can defend when they probe it.

The series in five sentences

  1. Architecture that isn't executable erodes: layers, contracts, budgets and accessibility checks fail the build. (01, 06, 07, 10)
  2. Signals hold state, RxJS handles events over time, idempotency makes retries safe. (02, 03)
  3. Native elements, managed focus and real routes keep the platform's behaviour, measured by real users. (04, 05, 06)
  4. The contract sits between frontend and backend; the server enforces auth, tenancy and long-running work. (07, 08, 09)
  5. Every change is small, tested at the right level, reversible, traceable and written down. (10, 11, 12)

Traps

  • Proposing a rewrite
  • Refactoring before the rule is executable
  • Describing debt as code quality
  • Settling an API argument in a meeting, not on the contract
  • The BFF as a second business-logic tier
  • Being the reviewer on every pull request
  • "We" all the way through, no result, or a story you didn't live
